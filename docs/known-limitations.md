# Known limitations

This is the v1 list of what is unfinished. None of it blocks the calculator. 0 at K 7, −1 at K 15 (using ln 0) and at K 17 (without ln 0), 2 at K 19, +iπ at K 21 (K 23 without ln 0), and −2 at K 25 (using ln 0) are proved minimal. Without ln 0, every K 25 program but two is excluded as −2. ln 2 at K 25, −e at K 17 (K 23 without ln 0), 1/e at K 17 (K 19 without ln 0), e + 1 at K 19 (K 21 without ln 0), and e² at K 21 are proved minimal as well. The last K 23 word for ln 2 is excluded by the phase deep.py computes in that same `decide.py --word` run. The proof is computer-assisted and is summarized in [docs/minimality.md](minimality.md). Every other length here is shortest known. A record that uses ln 0 = −∞ says so. No claim in this release depends on Schanuel's conjecture. A later identity that does will say so in the same place as the claim.

## Records

−iπ at K 45, 4, 2/3, −1/3, −3, and both 1/3 words are stored in `best-known.json`. −iπ at K 45 is not the K 21 word: that word is +iπ on this cut and −iπ under the principal log. −iπ at K 47 does not use ln 0. −iπ at K 45 and 1/3 at K 35 use ln 0 = −∞. 1/3 at K 37 does not. None of those lengths is proven minimal.

Scaled variable subtraction is K 565 and scaled variable addition is K 655. Both are the current constructions, shortest known, and both depend on ln 0 = −∞. Neither is proven minimal. +iπ at K 21, and at K 23 without ln 0, is proved minimal (tools/minimality/decide.py --open). −2 at K 25 with ln 0 is proved minimal (decide.py --open --deep: the tower programs that turn on a phase take tools/minimality/deep.py, runs of up to 5.5 million bits, the whole run takes about 80 minutes). Without ln 0, −2 at K 27 is shortest known: 2 K 25 programs remain undecided, exp(exp(exp(T4/z))) and exp(exp(T5/z)) (words `11E1E1111E1E1EEEE1E1E1E1E` and `11E1E1E1111E1E1EEEE1E1E1E`). Each is −2 only if the phase of a tower of towers, such as Im exp(T4/z) mod 2π, is within 10^(−1000) of ±π/2, and no enclosure reaches that phase. The other 16 that were undecided are excluded.

Phase-free infinities do not make subtraction or addition work at b = 0. sub(5, 0) and add(5, 0) are NaN in that model. b = 0 needs a directed infinity. artanh of a real greater than 1 is ½ ln|(1+x)/(1−x)| − iπ/2, matching mpmath's atanh. C99 catanh of that real with a +0 imaginary part returns +iπ/2.

## Float

Float is a hardware view of the same gates. It is not patched toward the exact value. The screen compares the real and imaginary parts separately. With 12 digits shown, a relative error of about 5×10^(−12) or larger on either component is flagged, and so is a component that float dropped. Within one working precision, a residual smaller than about 10^(−(digits−8)) relative to its operands is treated as 0. That precision starts at 120 digits and rises, up to 480, when the rounding would hide a value. It is not a fixed snap at 10^(−112). A known magnitude of 10^(−432) still resolves. 10^(−433) is past the cap, and the screen says it could not verify. A power such as 10^(−400) records its decade when that still fits in 480 digits, and division carries that decade, so ln(1+10^(−400)), 0−10^(−400), and 0−1/10^400 keep the small term instead of a confident 0. Past that cap, 0−10^(−500) is negation and still prints the value. A logarithm the circuit collapses, such as ln(1−10^(−500)) or ln(1±exp(−1000)), says it could not verify instead of a confident 0. An exact 0 times a finite factor stays a certified 0. A finite result far below a value past float range is refused unless an enclosure of the circuit confirms it: ln(10^400), ln(10^(−400)), √(4×10^(−400)), hypot(−10^(−200), 3×10^(−200)), hypot(10^160, −1), hypot(10^400, 10^400), hypot(10^(−400), 10^400), and (exp(800)+1)−exp(800) are certified; (exp(1200)+1)−exp(1200) is past the 480-digit cap and stays refused. A sum, difference, product, quotient, or small integer power of decimal and integer literals that comes to 0, such as 0.1 + 0.2 − 0.3, 1/10 + 2/10 − 3/10, 0.1·3 − 0.3, 0.5² − 0.25, 1/3 + 2/3 − 1, or (2/3)² − 4/9, is tracked as an exact rational in `facts.ts` and resolves to an exact 0 (up to 200-digit numerators and denominators). Deep towers whose extra term is absorbed past working precision (e.g. `111E1E1E1EE11EE`, which differs from −1 by about 10^(−1656520), or `1111EE1E1E1EE11EE`, which differs by about 4.4×10^(−116)) or whose intermediate exponential overflows/underflows the decimal range (e.g. `111E1E1E1E1EE1E`) are refused ("could not verify") rather than certified as exact integers. Words whose displayed imaginary part comes from the lower-cut convention on the negative real axis (e.g. `111111E1EEEE11E1E1EEE` and `ln(−1)`) carry the "on or very near the branch cut" note in exact mode as well as float mode. A compare that hits the node limit says the same, and a too-large circuit does not also say it left the finite numbers. A zero that a series shortcut produced is not certified. An infinity whose finite partner a gate drops, such as ln(ln(0))·i (−π + i∞) or exp of it (no limit), is not certified; so is any value computed from one, finite or not, even where it is right, such as 1/(ln(ln(0))·i).

These float results stay wrong, and the flag is what publishes that. There is no second float formula for them:

- A variable just off the cut, with |x| > 1 and |eps/x| ≤ 10^(−15), at 17 points of arcsin, arccos, and arsinh.
- A dropped component, including exp(10^(−9) i) − 1 and 10^300 + i. Scaling keeps (10^(−90) i) − 2×10^(−90) within the displayed digits.
- tan(50000), whose float and the exact value differ in the later of the 12 displayed digits, and exp(10^(−6)) − 1.
- A series shortcut keeps a leading term only when the next term is past the cap (|x| ≤ 10^(−241) for sin, tan, tanh, arctan, and cos(x)−1; decade ≤ −479, i.e. |x| < 10^(−478), for ln(1+x), exp(x)−1, and sqrt(1+x)−1, with the dropped x² error scale tracked so cancellations stay refused). Sin keeps odd powers for |x| ≤ 10^(−61). tan, tanh, and arctan keep odd powers, and cos(x)−1 keeps even powers, for |x| ≤ 10^(−20), until the next term is past the cap. A larger argument stays on the real circuit. tan(10^(−20)) is that series, so float and exact agree on the displayed digits. A one-digit literal such as tan(1e-5000) or exp(1e-1000)−1 prints that value, because the significand is 1 and the exponent is stored. The power spellings tan(10^(−5000)) and exp(10^(−1000))−1 do not resolve inside the cap, so they say could not verify rather than 0. cos(10^(−200))−1 keeps the even powers that start with −x²/2. Float still underflows that square, so the flag stays.
- Variable asinh of ±10^(−12), which leaves a float residual of about 10^(−15). Below about 10^(−12) the low-precision flag is also set.
- tan(π/2) and tan(3π/2), labelled near a pole in both modes. The digits of a near-pole float still depend on the engine.
- sin, cos, and tan of a real at least 10^6, because float π cannot reduce them.
- A real part around 1.3×10^154 or larger, where the shared scale overflows and x − y is undefined.
- ln(−2 − 10^(−20) i) and ln(−2 − 10^(−35) i), which show +iπ in float and −iπ in exact and are flagged as near the cut.

What the flag does not catch:

- A relative error at or below about 5×10^(−12) on a component. That is one digit past the 12 on screen. A few ulps, and 0.1 + 0.2 − 0.3, sit in this band.
- 0 minus the smallest positive float, 5×10^(−324). Float keeps a signed zero and exact keeps 0. The comparison treats those magnitudes as equal.
- 1 − 10^400 is NaN in float. That is overflow, not a value the flag replaces.
- A cancellation whose size is not visible in a known leaf and not visible as a residual at 120 digits can still agree with float. The reference does not search every expression for a hidden scale.

## Exact mode: wrong values still certified, and refusals that are too strict

A fuzz of about 7,800 expressions against mpmath on 2026-11-25, plus an additional 2,628 consecutive certified fuzz expressions across Seeds 1–8 against 2,500-digit mpmath (in-process and through the worker wire), found **0 wrong certified values**. The six wrong values listed for 1.0.3 are fixed or refused, the second-order and tiny-argument cases from 1.0.4 are fixed or refused (`tanh(10^(−122) i) − 10^(−122) i` and `ln(tanh(10^(−236) + 10^(−236) i))` certify accurately; `(arctan(−3×10^(−122) i) + 3×10^(−122) i)·10^100`, `sqrt(1 + 10^(−250)) − 1 − 10^(−250)/2`, and `exp(10^(−237)) − 1 − 10^(−237)` are refused), and `v1.0.9`–`v1.0.10` closed `atan(±i)` pole checks beyond pointer identity (`1 + exp(−atan(√−1))`), O(n²) BigInt compilation hangs on `1e-999999`, and series-shortcut error propagation across cancellations and amplification (`userSum`, `tMul`, `tDiv`, `expGates`, `tLn`, `tPow`, `tArcsin`). No known-wrong certified expressions remain.

Of the expressions that became refused under the stricter absorbed-term checks, all 10 — `10^(−500) + 10^400`, `hypot(10^400, 10^400)`, `hypot(10^(−400), 10^400)`, `avg(10^(−400), 10^400)`, `hypot(exp(700), 10^350)`, `arsinh(exp(−1000) + 2)`, `arctan(exp(−800)) − π/2`, `cosh(0.7 − 10^(−400))`, `(exp(−300) + 10^(−20) + 10^(−400)) − exp(−300)`, and `exp(3.5×10^(−479)) − 1` — are now certified again (via the ball enclosure and the extended leading-term series shortcut with exact-decimal decade tracking; cancellations exposing the dropped x² term and power spellings past the cap such as `exp(3.5·10^(−479)) − 1` stay refused). A refusal is not a wrong answer.

## Circuits and search

- Rebuilding a circuit from its press string preserves low-precision, pole, and large-angle flags across `isolateCircuit` clears and fresh browser sessions via the persisted structural precision-note registry in `lib/eml/node.ts` (`localStorage`). A brand-new device with an empty storage that loads an uncompiled raw press string sees only the gates.
- The fast suite stops at the K = 21 table (K 23 without ln 0). `npm run eml:golf` runs K = 35 in about 75 s and is not part of the publish check; its last run is in [docs/golf-k35.md](golf-k35.md).
- The constant search does not merge circuits by value. Its pool is every float-finite circuit that did not overflow or underflow, 88 M through K 35 with ln 0, so K 37 and past are left to tools/emlsearch.
- 0, −1, and 2 tie the lengths in arXiv:2603.21852. ±iπ at K 21 was posted publicly before this project. 3 at K 29 ties an earlier code-golf answer. Those are not claimed as first discoveries. The citations are in the EML and One README.
