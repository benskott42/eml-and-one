## Engineering Next (2026-11-27 review)

1. **Ball arithmetic as the exact engine** ([design/ball-engine.md](design/ball-engine.md), design only). The point engine's certification is a stack of heuristics (raise, re-read, scrub, snap, refuse), and each fuzz round has found an input between them. Balls prove digits by construction. Migrate in stages behind the golden gate, with the nightly fuzz (now checking each part) as the net.
2. **Exact-engine speed.** `(−5 − 10^(−300) i) − (10^(−236) + 10^(−236) i)` is a 404-gate circuit and takes about 14 s: three full re-reads at 357, 456, and 480 digits from `hiddenComponent`, `refusedResult`, and `squaredTinyPart`. Item 1 replaces those passes with one adaptive loop. A 2026-11-27 profile of `10^(−240) − atanh(exp(800))` (348 distinct gates, 17 s): about 20 ms per gate at 480 digits, nearly all of it decimal.js multiplication inside `exp`/`ln`/`cos`; a 456-digit re-read (absorbed term) and a 480-digit stability probe at 6–7 s each; and a 4 s ball confirmation. The probe compares two precisions on purpose, so the per-precision cache cannot share them. Balls (item 1) remove the re-reads; faster high-precision `exp`/`ln` would cut the rest.
3. **Link the remaining compiled operations.** `Eml.AppSub` and `Eml.AppOps` prove the compiler's own words for 19 operators and ten trigonometric words composed from them with `Tok.subst`. Linked: `sigma`, `sinh`, `cosh`, `artanh`, `arcosh`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `tanh`, and `arctan`. `sinh_correct'` and `cosh_correct'` hold for every `a`. `sin` is read by `valueOf` (a zero value may be `−0`); `tan` needs `cos x ≠ 0`; `tanh` needs `exp(2x) + 1 ≠ 0`; `arctan` is `−i·log(√(1+i·x)/√(1−i·x))` for `x ≠ ±i`, which disagrees with Mathlib's `arctan` on the ray `{iy : y < −1}`. `i` is `exp(iπ/2)` (K 57), proved by hand. `certifyDir` decides `1/2` (`half_dir`, `decide +kernel`, 2.7 s), `10`, and `π`. `i`, `−i`, `2i`, and `10i` are decided only (`i_dir`, `negi_dir`, `twoi_dir`, `tenI_dir`: `(certifyDir _).isSome`, so `certifyDir_sound` applies) and are not named. `arccos_value_eq_ext` and `extEmlArccos_eq_word` derive `hsum`. Still unlinked: the compiled token words for `arcsin`, `arsinh`, and `arccos` (an `arccos` word goes through `Tok.subst`). On `*_correct_toC'`, the only `≠ −0` hypotheses left are the exact forms of `twoMul` and `divTwo` and the infinite-output cases (`ln`, `inv`, division by `0`, `pow` at `0`). `exp` accepts `−0`.
4. **Complex boxes for the certificate checker.** `Eml.Cert.certify` certifies 24 of the 43 closed stored words in `ℂ`, including `-ipi-strict`, and `Eml.Cert.certifyExt` the other 19 in `EmlExt`, including `pi` and `-ipi`. Check 114 runs the TypeScript mirror; the kernel decision is in `scripts/lean-bridge-check.ts`. `Eml.CertBoxC` has the integer layer (interval product and quotient, the clamp into `[-1, 1]`, square-root bounds on the modulus), the sharp logarithm `lnSLo` / `lnSHi`, cosine and sine at one point (`trigPt`, for `|fx t| ≤ 4`), cosine and sine on an interval (`trigI`), and arcsin bounds (`asinLo` / `asinHi`). `trigI` reduces by an integer multiple of `2π` and, when the reduced endpoint is outside `[-4, 4]`, returns the trivial box `[-1, 1]`. The arcsin bisection is kept only when `trigPt` certifies it; otherwise the bound is `±π/2`. `argLoOf` / `argHiOf` bound `arg` on a box that stays off the nonpositive real axis: `arcsin(im / ‖z‖)` when the real part is positive, and `±(π/2 − arcsin(re / ‖z‖))` when the imaginary part is strictly signed. `bExp` and `bLn` cover a nonzero imaginary part: `exp` multiplies the real exponential by cosine and sine, and `ln` is `log ‖z‖ + i arg` off the nonpositive real axis. The real logarithm uses the sharp bisection. `dirConst_ten` and `dirConst_pi` name the certificates of `10` and `π`. The symbol rules for `−exp` are still open, so the compiled `arcsin`, `arsinh`, and `arccos` words stay unlinked.
