## Engineering Next (2026-11-27 review)

1. **Ball arithmetic as the exact engine** ([design/ball-engine.md](design/ball-engine.md), design only). The point engine's certification is a stack of heuristics (raise, re-read, scrub, snap, refuse), and each fuzz round has found an input between them. Balls prove digits by construction. Migrate in stages behind the golden gate, with the nightly fuzz (now checking each part) as the net.
2. **Exact-engine speed.** A profile of the two slow circuits showed about 70% of the time in decimal.js schoolbook multiplication (`P.mul`) inside `exp`, `ln`, `cos` and `sin` at a few hundred digits. `lib/eml/fast-mul.ts` computes that same integer product and rounds it the same way, so the digits are unchanged. Five cold `exactReport` runs on this machine, median, timing only the evaluation: `(−5 − 10^(−300) i) − (10^(−236) + 10^(−236) i)` 14.75 s → 9.74 s, and `10^(−240) − atanh(exp(800))` 13.62 s → 8.43 s. What remains is converting the product back into base-1e7 limbs, and the schoolbook division those series still do. The re-reads are unchanged: the first circuit still re-reads at 357, 456 and 480 digits (`hiddenComponent`, `refusedResult`, `squaredTinyPart`), and the second still compares two precisions on purpose, so the per-precision cache cannot share them. Balls (item 1) remove those passes.
3. **Link the remaining compiled operations.** `Eml.AppSub` and `Eml.AppOps` prove the compiler's own words for 19 operators (`x ± y`, `x · y`, `x / y`, `−x`, `x²`, `x³`, `x^y`, `e^x`, `2x`, `x/2`, `x ± 1`, `x − 2`, `1/x`, `√x`, `exp`, `ln`) for all complex inputs; check 115 pins each word. `x − c` for other constants uses the same rotation (`appRot_finite` covers any finite `c`) but some constants take a shortcut variant (`x − e` is K 129, not 137), and `CertDir` gives any certified constant its `EmlDir` value. The trigonometric words are 2,000–10,000 tokens and the next large target.
4. **Complex boxes for the certificate checker.** `Eml.Cert.certify` certifies 23 of the 43 closed stored words in `ℂ` and `Eml.Cert.certifyExt` 17 more in `EmlExt` (check 114). `pi`, `-ipi` and `-ipi-strict` need `exp` and `ln` of boxes with nonzero imaginary parts, which need proved `cos`/`sin`/`arg` bounds.

## Mathematical / Search Open Problems (No Code Fix Expected)
- **`−2` without `ln 0` at K 27:** Only two K 25 programs remain (`exp(exp(exp(T4/z)))` and `exp(exp(T5/z))`). As documented in [minimality.md](minimality.md), numerical enclosure requires ~10^1656519–10^1656521 bits and standard unconditional transcendence theorems do not apply; both are excluded conditionally under Schanuel's conjecture. Settling this unconditionally requires a new transcendence result.
- **Shorter records past K 41:** `−iπ` (K 45 / K 47 without `ln 0`), `4` (K 39), `2/3` (K 39), `−1/3` (K 41), `−3` (K 35), `1/3` (K 35 / K 37), and scaled subtraction/addition (K 565 / K 655) remain shortest known, not proved minimal.
